tiny noise

A sound toy. Fiddle with your own sound recordings, data, gestures, and scanned drawings for maximized fun with noise.

Quick manual

Getting started

Open Start from an example and pick one, or work on the lanes that are already there. Press Start (or Space) to hear all lanes together. Each lane is one voice: it plays one sound on its own timing, and any change you make is heard straight away. Labels with a dotted underline, and the menus in each lane's header, explain themselves when you point at them.

The top bar

  • Start and Record: Record captures exactly what you hear until you press it again, then downloads it as WAV (uncompressed, works everywhere) or MP3 (much smaller), chosen in the menu next to it. MP3 needs an internet connection the first time, to load the encoder; without one it falls back to WAV.
  • Tempo: 10 to 300 beats per minute; type decimals for fine changes.
  • Master volume: overall level. Most of the slider covers normal levels; type a number for exact values.
  • Auto level (on by default): slowly evens out the overall loudness, so a dense noise bed and a few sparse clicks end up at a similar volume and you rarely need to touch the volume. Untick it to hear the raw differences.
  • Safety limiter (on by default): catches sudden loud peaks before they reach your ears and speakers. Unticking it turns the label red; keep the volume low.
  • Save project (.json) and Open project (.json): see Saving your work.

A lane, from top to bottom

  • Header: the on/off box (or keys 1–8), the Sound menu, the Timing menu, the Rate (how long each step is, from 2 bars down to 1/32, with triplets, quintuplets and septuplets) and the number of steps in the loop (up to 32). Remove deletes the lane.
  • Squares: click to cycle off, soft, medium, accent. They are grouped by beat, one bar per row. While playing, a small dot in each square shows where the last hit really landed: centred means exactly on time.
  • Pattern: the same steps as text: . off, 1 soft, 2 medium, 3 or x accent. The number of characters sets the loop length.
  • Tools: Euclid spreads a number of hits evenly; All steps on/off; Clear; Whole sound plays the entire recording or drawing from each lit step; Move off the grid lets you drag single steps earlier or later, or type offsets in percent of a step.
  • Accents: a second pattern with its own length: > louder, - softer, . unchanged. Because its length differs from the loop, the emphasis moves: >.. over 16 steps lands somewhere new every bar.
  • Volume and Length (how long each hit lasts, 1 to 2000 ms) are always visible. Everything else is under More settings.

More settings

At the top are the main knobs: Rough (grit and unstable pitch), Dark (below 50 thinner, above 50 more muffled), Dense (below 50 skips hits, above 50 adds quick repeats), Loose (0 is exact timing; higher lets hits land early or late; this is the only control that randomises timing) and Analog ↔ digital (towards 0 warm, wobbly and with a faint hum while playing; 50 clean; towards 100 crushed and hard). Below them: frequency or playback speed, attack, pitch drop, filter, drive, pan, delay and wander (a slow Bluetooth-like drift), shifting the lane earlier or later, delaying every second step, soft-to-accent contrast, chance, ratchets, rotation, loop length, recording start point and scatter, playing a recording backwards, and Moved by another lane, where another lane's hits briefly push one of this lane's knobs.

Timing modes

  • Steps: the grid as you set it.
  • Turing loop: the grid slowly rewrites itself; Change sets how fast.
  • Evolve: each loop the pattern grows into a new one by a cellular-automaton rule; Reseed starts over.
  • Rain: hits at random moments with no grid; you set how many per second.
  • Bounce: a dropped ball: hits get faster and quieter as it settles, then it is dropped again.
  • Cloud: a continuous texture of tiny grains moving through a sound. With a recording it can stretch time, freeze one moment, or run backwards.

Sounds

The Sound menu has Beeps, Noise and Library sound. The Library holds built-in Field sounds (synthesized imitations of rain, wind, water, fire, gravel, paper, radio static, vinyl, tape hiss and a distant city; not real recordings), clean Digital noise (1-bit, velvet, 4-bit, shift-register, glitch), sounds made in the Data panel, your recordings, your drawings, the microphone and saved loops. Choose Load your own recording… in any lane's menu, or drag audio files onto the page; they stay on your computer. Noise sounds are set a little quieter than beeps by default, because noise sounds louder at the same level.

Panels (below the lanes)

  • Play: the XY pad changes two things at once on all lanes (and can spring back); Mutate nudges everything at random and Undo goes back; Drift lets the main knobs wander by themselves; Morph slides between two saved scenes; Echo.
  • Room: the microphone (as a sound for any lane, or fed back to the speakers), a looper that wears down a little on each pass, and the camera painting into a drawing.
  • Data: turns numbers, text or any file into exact clicks, pure tones, noise bursts or a raw waveform, with a barcode view. Keep the result as a Library sound or add it as a lane.
  • Spectrogram and drawing: the top shows what you hear (time left to right, pitch bottom to top). Paint below and it becomes sound in three ways: Noise inks (colour picks the kind of noise), Texture (grains, no pitch) and Pitch (spectrogram style). Live scan loops the drawing; press and drag to hear the spot under your pointer. Add as lane cuts a drawing into one slice per step.
  • Score: save the current state as a scene, list scenes with a number of bars to make a song, and play it, with or without recording it.
  • Your recordings: the audio files you have loaded.

Saving your work

Save project (.json) downloads one .json file with everything: the lanes as they are now (patterns included, as readable text), all scenes and the song, drawings, data sounds and your recordings. Open project (.json), or dragging the file onto the page, brings it all back. The page also keeps an autosave in this browser every 15 seconds; Restore last session appears inside Start from an example when there is one (recordings are only kept in saved project files). Record saves the sound itself.

Keys

Space start or stop · 1–8 mute a lane · Q–I hit a lane once · hold S to stutter · M mutate · Z undo (also after loading an example).

Tips

  • Slow, minimal and tight: low tempo, a fine rate (1/32, quintuplets), short Length (3–20 ms), few hits placed just off the beat, Loose at 0 (dots centred), Analog ↔ digital above 50. The Slow and tight and Data pulse examples start there.
  • Syncopation without a groove template: give lanes different loop lengths (5, 7, 11…) or rates, rotate patterns, and use short accent patterns. See the Polymeter example.
  • Textures: a Library recording in a Cloud lane with low Travel speed gives a slowly moving bed; see Rain room and Calibration.
  • Microphone feedback can get loud quickly even with the limiter: start low.

Room: microphone, looper and camera

Room brings in the microphone, a looper that wears down with each pass, and the camera as a live drawing.

Microphone

Listens to the room. Any lane can use "Microphone (last 8 s)" as its sound, and the room can be sent back to the speakers for feedback. Start the feedback low: it grows quickly, and the safety limiter only catches peaks.

Degrading looper

Grabs the last few seconds of everything you heard and loops it, a little more worn on every pass, like a tape wearing out.

Camera as a drawing

Paints the camera image into the current drawing many times a second and turns on Live scan, so you hear what it sees. Point it at paper, hands or light.

Data: turn numbers, text and files into sound

Paste numbers or any text, load any file, or pick a ready-made source. The data is read as bits, values or raw samples and played as exact clicks, pure tones or noise, in the spirit of Ryoji Ikeda's data pieces. The barcode shows what is being played.

Spectrogram and drawing

Spectrogram and drawing shows what you hear and turns what you paint into sound.

What you hear: time runs left to right (newest on the right, about 8.5 seconds shown), pitch runs bottom to top, brightness is loudness.

What you draw: paint on the black canvas and it becomes sound. Left to right is always time, and bright marks are loud. Choose how the rest is read under the canvas: Noise inks lets you paint with eight colours, each a different noise (thicker marks are louder, sprayed or speckled marks break the noise up, and a colour photo becomes a noise collage); Texture turns marks into grains of noise or of your recordings (dots become hits, lines become streams, scribbles become crackle, and up means grain size, tone, density or position in a recording), while Pitch reads the canvas like a spectrogram, with up meaning higher notes. Press Live scan and the drawing loops continuously, so every stroke is heard at once. Press and drag on the canvas to hold and scrub: the sound under your pointer sustains while you paint. The blue-green glow on the canvas is what actually comes out of the speakers as the playhead passes.
